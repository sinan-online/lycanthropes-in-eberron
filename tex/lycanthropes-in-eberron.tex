\chapter*{Lycanthropes in Eberron}

What if werewolves of Eberron were twelve different ``tribes'', each impacted by a different moon of Eberron?\par

This book is inspired by
\href{https://drive.google.com/file/d/1XxNb80pKTArS2SecLkFQE3nnbXTwt2j9/view}{the homebrewed lunar calendar of Eberron by abexy},
the classical World of Darkness game, Werewolf: the Apocalypse by White Wolf,
and not the least, \href{https://keith-baker.com/2025-lycanthropes/}{this blog post from Keith Baker}.\par

The rules are intended for players to play as lycanthropes, specifically werewolves, in Eberron campaigns.

\begin{DndSidebar}[float=!b]{Power \& Intention}
    These rules are intentionally more powerful than a standard D\&D 5e class.
    They are designed for a party composed entirely of hereditary werewolves --- one from each moon, acting as a pack.
    The different moons provide complementary abilities: a Zarantyr werewolf brings storm and wrath, a Therendor werewolf brings healing, a Lharvion werewolf brings detection, and so on.
    The pack as a whole is the unit of power, not any single character.

    Playing this in a mixed party --- some werewolves, some not --- will create significant power imbalances and is not recommended without DM adjustment.

    Werewolves are physically powerful, but struggle with control over their transformations and instincts, and are actively hunted by the Silver Flame.
    This is in the spirit of the personal horror themes of W:tA and the struggle to maintain one's humanity.

    Central to this tension is the werewolf's relationship with human society.
    Hereditary werewolves must breed with humans to sustain their bloodlines, yet humans can be prey, quarry, or beloved companions.
    This contradiction --- needing humanity while standing apart from it, protecting a world that would destroy them if it knew what they were --- is the engine of the intended roleplay experience.
\end{DndSidebar}

The intended audience is DMs and troupes familiar with the Eberron setting.
The DM's mau find inspiration, particularly for in-depth NPCs, or for a different one-shot or campaign involving an all-werewolf pack.\par

\section*{New Rules of Lycanthropy}

In D\&D 5e, lycanthropy is a curse or disease that can be contracted through a bite or scratch from a lycanthrope.
In this version, lycanthropy comes in two forms: a \textbf{hereditary} form passed down through bloodlines, and a \textbf{contracted} form that is caught like a disease.
If it is hereditary, the character is born with lycanthropy, and this cannot be removed, short of a \emph{wish} spell.\par

\subsection*{Hereditary Lycanthropy}
Hereditary lycanthropes are born to one werewolf parent and one parent who is an ordinary Medium humanoid.
Inheritance is not guaranteed --- the blood may or may not pass to the child --- but it cannot be diluted: a character either has hereditary lycanthropy or they do not.
The probability and inheritance rules are detailed in the Blood Inheritance section below, after the forms have been described.

A character typically does not know they carry the blood until their passage into adulthood.
At that threshold, the first shift comes on its own --- involuntary, disorienting, unmistakable.
The werewolf parent is charged with being present for this moment, or finding the child swiftly after, and guiding them to their tribe.
It is the parent's responsibility to introduce the young werewolf ``cub'' to the traditions of their moon, to teach them the first deliberate shift, and to bring them before the tribe's elders.\par

Mechanically, this is the point at which a player character gains their first level in the Werewolf Class.
From there, the character may take additional werewolf levels whenever they level up, or they may focus on their base class --- the blood does not demand constant training, only acknowledgement.\par

Werewolves, the most common type of hereditary lycanthropes, have an affinity to one of the twelve moons of Eberron, which influences their lycanthropic form and abilities.
Werewolves of the same moon tend to form close-knit packs or tribes, often centered around shared beliefs and customs related to their moon.\par

Through parent to cub, werewolves pass down not only their lycanthropic nature but also their traditions.
Each one of the moons correspond to a tribe, and the traditions are detailed below.\par

Finally, werewolves with hereditary lycanthropy have control over their transformations, and are able to hold in-between forms as well - five forms in total.
Only Medium humanoids may be hereditary lycanthropes.\par

In the cosmology of Eberron, hereditary werewolves are guardians of Eberron, with powers granted to them by Siberys.
They are bound to Eberron itself --- tied to its moons above and instinctively opposed to the corruption that rises from Khyber below.
This is how a parent will explain their world view to cubs.
How correct this belief is, and whether this has comological implications is campaign-specific and is left to the DM.\par


\begin{DndSidebar}[float=!b]{Design Intent \& Inspirations}
    Keith Baker's \href{https://keith-baker.com/2025-lycanthropes/}{2025 blog post on lycanthropes} identifies hereditary werewolves as ``alphas'' and presents several possibilities for how they might fit into Eberron.
    This book takes a specific direction among those possibilities: drawing on the werewolf traditions in Werewolf: the Apocalypse, hereditary werewolves are guardians of Eberron --- protectors of the natural order, aligned with the moons of Siberys, and resisting to the corruption of Khyber.

    This is a deliberate synthesis of Baker's Eberron framework and White Wolf's tradition of werewolves as cosmic guardians caught between two worlds.
    The result is a class built for personal horror, tribal politics, and the ongoing struggle to remain human enough to live alongside the people you are called to protect --- and sometimes prey upon.

    Baker identifies six distinct strains of lycanthropy in Eberron.
    This book maps most closely to two: \textbf{Olarune's Blessed} (nature champions with a gift rather than a curse) for the guardian framework, and \textbf{The Changing Folk} (fey beings of Thelanis) for the Rhaan tribe specifically.
    The strains Baker calls Dyrrn's Corrupted and the Curse of the Wild Heart --- both transmissible, neither aligned with guardianship --- are treated here as the origins of contracted lycanthropy.

    Hereditary lycanthropy is intentionally more powerful than a standard 5e class.
    It makes most sense to play with a full party of hereditary lycanthropes, similar to W:tA, so that the power level and the themes remain consistent across the table.
\end{DndSidebar}

\subsection*{Contracted Lycanthropy}
A character bitten or scratched by a lycanthrope (hereditary or contracted) must make a DC 12 Constitution saving throw.
On a failed save, the character ``is cursed'' with lycanthropy, as per the original rules.\par

They cannot pass this on to their offspring, even if they become a lycanthrope themselves.
They can, however ``infect'' others through bites and scratches, as per the original rules.\par


\begin{DndSidebar}[float=!b]{Contracted Lycanthropy}
    The rules in this book are written for consistency with the original D\&D 5e werewolf.
    The 5e monster stat block for werewolves roughly corresponds to the Hybrid form detailed below, for an NPC of the 8th level.
    This means that DMs can use the original werewolf stat block for NPC lycanthropes without creating immersion-breaking inconsistencies.
    If a player wants their character to have the contracted lycanthropy ``curse'', they can continue their usual character progression and can access the feats in the Feats section.
\end{DndSidebar}

If contracted werewolves embrace their lycanthropy through one of the methods detailed right below, they gain a level in the Werewolf Class and can continue levelling up in this.
They gain all the benefits and drawbacks of lycanthropy, including the five forms detailed below.\par

\paragraph*{Rite of Olarune}
Under rare circumstances, a contracted lycanthrope may undergo the \emph{Rite of Olarune}, a druidic ritual that transforms the contracted curse into true hereditary lycanthropy.
The rite is cast as a ritual over the course of one hour, performed under a full moon, and requires the willing participation of the subject --- it cannot be performed on an unwilling creature.
Upon completion, the lycanthropy is no longer a curse.
It becomes part of the character's blood.
The character may then take levels in the Werewolf Class.
Their moon is determined by whichever of the twelve moons is fullest at the moment the ritual concludes.\par

The rite is known to \textbf{Oalian the Greatpine}, the ancient awakened archdruid of the Eldeen Reaches.
At the DM's discretion, a \textbf{Moonspeaker} elder may also be able to perform it, though such knowledge is rare even among their order.\par

\paragraph*{The Traveler}
At the DM's discretion, a contracted werewolf who enters a pact with \textbf{the Traveler} may receive the same transformation as a patron boon.
The Traveler, one of the Dark Six, is the sovereign of change and transformation and is revered by lycanthropes who view their condition as a gift rather than a curse.
The Traveler may offer to ``complete'' the gift in exchange for service --- though the Traveler's gifts rarely cost what they appear to.\par

Mechanically, they can take levels in the werewolf class.
Their moon is the one that was fullest during the time when the pact with the Traveller was signed.
While during regular play, they will look like any other hereditary werewolf.
However, they may have distinctions that pop up from time to time, based on the needs of the campaign.
For example, because their connection to the moon is weak, they may not receive the advantage and disadvantage to their rolls to shift.
Or their nature might reveal that there is something off whenever there is magical scrying on them.\par

\paragraph*{Dyrrn the Corruptor}
At the DM's discretion, \textbf{Dyrrn the Corruptor} --- the daelkyr whose influence gave rise to the first contracted strains of lycanthropy --- may offer a darker version of the same transformation as a warlock pact boon.
Where Olarune's rite cleanses, Dyrrn's version seals the curse deep into the blood.
Mechanically, they can take levels in the werewolf class.
Furthermore, they can choose features from all of the moons - whatever they like.
However, spiritually, their connection is not to the moons that are in Syberis, but to Khyber.
This means that other hereditary werewolves will typically be able to tell what they are through some sort of visible or olfactory hint.
Maybe their growls are eerie, or maybe their eyes shine with a tint, or their elemental magic has a tainted aura.
Second, whenever they use a power, they are immediately revealed as an ``unnatural'' being.
Third, they do not receive any advantages or disadvantages to shifting based on the moon phase.
Fourth, when they need to roll against Involuntary Shifting (see below), they do so at a disadvantage.\par

\begin{DndSidebar}[float=!b]{Who Are the Dark Pack?}
The \textbf{Dark Pack of Droaam} --- Zaeurl's nomadic band of werewolves and worgs, sworn enemies of the Silver Flame and loyal allies of the Daughters of Sora Kell --- predates this book's twelve-tribe framework, and its exact composition is left to the DM.

\textbf{Mixed survivors (recommended).} The Pack's founding members were purge survivors from several different moons, who no longer identify strongly with the tribe traditions they were born into after those traditions failed to protect them.
New members --- typically contracted lycanthropes the Pack chooses not to kill --- are offered the same choice as any contracted werewolf: complete the transformation through a pact with the Traveler or with Dyrrn the Corruptor (see above).
A gentler Dark Pack leans on the Traveler; a darker one leans on Dyrrn.
Either reading keeps the Pack's membership varied, and ties its recruitment directly into the pact-boon rules a contracted player character might use themselves.

\textbf{A single bloodline.} Alternatively, a DM may prefer to make the whole Pack one thing, for a cleaner story:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \emph{All Dyrrn's Corrupted} --- their ruthlessness, and the fact that other hereditary werewolves can sense something is wrong with them, is daelkyr taint rather than zeal. Good for a campaign that wants the Pack to be a monster, not a tragedy.
    \item \emph{All Zarantyr} --- the Pack becomes what Storm and Chaos werewolves look like once every restraint has burned away under grief. Good for a campaign where the party's own Zarantyr packmate has to reckon with what they could become.
    \item \emph{All Sypheros} --- the Pack becomes living proof of exactly what the other eleven tribes already fear about Mabar's tribe. Good for a campaign built on Sypheros's ongoing struggle to be trusted.
\end{itemize}

Based on the campaign needs, feel free to mix-and-match.
\end{DndSidebar}\par

\section*{The Forms}

The five forms represent more than physical transformation --- they mark points on the spectrum between a werewolf's two natures.
In the Humanoid Form, they pass unnoticed through civilized society; in the Animal Form, they are wholly of the wild.
The three forms between are expressions of the struggle itself: powerful and instinctual, never fully free of either pull.
For hereditary werewolves, this is not an affliction imposed from outside --- it is the defining tension of their existence.

A lycanthrope has five forms:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Humanoid Form:} The lycanthrope appears as a normal humanoid of their race.
    \item \textbf{Shifter Form:} The lycanthrope takes on a mix of humanoid and animal features, gaining some of the abilities of their animal form. In this form, they resemble Shifters.
    \item \textbf{Hybrid Form:} This is the hybrid form from D\&D 5e.
    \item \textbf{Dire Form:} The lycanthrope takes on a larger and more powerful version of their animal form, gaining enhanced abilities.
    \item \textbf{Animal Form:} The lycanthrope fully transforms into their animal form.
\end{itemize}

Contracted lycanthropes have no control over their transformations and will shift into their Hybrid form during a full moon.
However, hereditary lycanthropes have control over their shifts and can choose to shift into any of the five forms.\par

To shift between these forms, the lycanthrope must use an action on their turn.
They can shift only to the next form up or down the list as one action.
For example, they can shift from Humanoid to Shifter, or from Hybrid to Dire, but not Humanoid to Hybrid in a single action --- that takes two turns.\par

This transformation, ``shifting'', requires a Wisdom check. If their moon is dark (new moon), the check is made with disadvantage.
If their moon is full, the check is made with advantage.
The DC of the check is 18 - the character level in the werewolf class/2 (rounded down), as per the table below.
This means that early on, the characters will not be able to rely on their shifting abilities as much, outside of the full moon phase.
To determine the phase of the moon, either use a d10, with 5 being new moon and 0 being full moon,
or use the amazing \href{https://drive.google.com/file/d/1XxNb80pKTArS2SecLkFQE3nnbXTwt2j9/view}{the homebrewed lunar calendar of Eberron by abexy}.


\begin{DndTable}[header=Shifting DC by Level]{XX}
    Level  & Shifting DC \\
    1-2    & 17          \\
    3-4    & 16          \\
    5-6    & 15          \\
    7-8    & 14          \\
    9-10   & 13          \\
    11-12  & 12          \\
    13-14  & 11          \\
    15-16  & 10          \\
    17-18  & 9           \\
    19-20  & 8           \\
\end{DndTable}

The transformation is a magical effect. On the turn, this will register as such on spells like \emph{detect magic}.
It can be suppressed by \emph{antimagic field} or similar effects, but not dispelled.
In other words, the transformation cannot take place in an \emph{antimagic field},
but the werewolf that has already completed the transformation remains in
the form that they transformed into.
At the DM's discretion, \emph{counterspell} may be used to prevent a lycanthrope from shifting if cast as a reaction before they use their action to shift.
Treat the shift as a spell whose level is given by the Spell Level column in the Werewolf Class table.
Alternatively, the roll may be made at a disadvantage.
The lycanthrope can try again on their next turn.\par

\begin{DndSidebar}[float=!b]{Shifting \& 2024 Rules}
    The 2024 rules make the werewolf transformation a bonus action.
    If using the 2024 ruleset, ``shifting'' is a bonus action.
\end{DndSidebar}

Each one of the forms come with their own set of abilities and traits, which are detailed in the next sections for werewolves.

\begin{DndSidebar}[float=!b]{Silver Vulnerability \& 2024 Rules}
    The 2024 revision of D\&D 5e removed vulnerability to silvered weapons from the werewolf stat block.
    This book intentionally restores it.
    The Silver Flame's persecution of lycanthropes and the power of silver as a supernatural bane are central to the setting and to the personal horror themes of this homebrew.
    Removing silver vulnerability would diminish both the threat posed by werewolf hunters and the dread of knowing exactly what can kill you.
    DMs who prefer the 2024 approach may remove this vulnerability at their discretion.
\end{DndSidebar}

\subsection*{Humanoid Form}
In this form, the werewolf appears as a normal humanoid of their race.
\paragraph*{Exhaustion} When shifting back to this form from any other form, the werewolf suffers one level of exhaustion.
\paragraph*{Vulnerability} Werewolves in all forms have vulnerability to silver and silvered weapons.


\subsection*{Shifter Form}\label{sec:shifter-form}
In this form, the werewolf resembles a Longtooth Shifter with relatively heavy build and will likely be mistaken for one.
\footnote{If a check is needed, the DC is 19 to identify them as a lycanthrope rather than a Shifter on a Nature (Wis) or Arcana (Int) check. However, unless there is a specific reason to suspect a lycanthrope, people will assume they are a Shifter without any checks.}

When shifting from the Humanoid Form to the Shifter Form, the lycantrope does not lose their items or clothing, unless there is something particular about the item or the clothes (really tight-fitting silk clothes, crystals, fragile items, delicate and tight fabrics, etc).

\paragraph*{Attribute Bonuses} Their Strength increases by 2, up to a maximum of 25, and their Dexterity increases by 1, also up to a maximum of 25.
\paragraph*{Damage Resistance} They gain resistance to bludgeoning, piercing, and slashing damage from nonmagical weapons that aren't silvered.
\paragraph*{Vulnerability} Werewolves in all forms have vulnerability to silver and silvered weapons.
\paragraph*{Claw Attack} Their unarmed strikes deal 1d6 slashing damage, plus their strength modifier. They count as magical for the purpose of overcoming resistance and immunity to nonmagical attacks and damage. They are proficient with this attack.

% \paragraph*{Bite Attack} They can use their action to make a bite attack, dealing 1d6 piercing damage plus their strength modifier. They are proficient with this attack. This attack can also transmit lycanthropy if the target fails a DC 12 Constitution saving throw.
% \paragraph*{Proficiency Bonus} They gain proficiency with Perception skills.

\begin{DndSidebar}[float=!b]{Shifters \& The Shifter Form}
    This form is intended to compliment the Eberron campaign lore about Shifters being hunted down during the Silver Purge, and the prejudices against them.
\end{DndSidebar}

\subsection*{Hybrid Form}
This is the majestic, impressive werewolf look - it is the hybrid form from D\&D 5e.

When shifting from the Shifter Form to the Hybrid Form, the lycantrope loses their items. They are either torn, or dropped to the ground.
Magical rings, necklaces, amulets are an exception, as well as some loose-fitting items, such as cloaks and scarves.\par

\paragraph*{Attribute Bonuses} Their Strength increases by 6 from their human form, up to a maximum of 25, and their Dexterity increases by 3, also up to a maximum of 25.
\paragraph*{Damage Immunity} They gain immunity to bludgeoning, piercing, and slashing damage from nonmagical weapons that aren't silvered.
\paragraph*{Vulnerability} Werewolves in all forms have vulnerability to silver and silvered weapons.
\paragraph*{Lack of Speech} They lose the ability to speak, and social checks other than Intimidation automatically fail.
Magical communication such as \emph{tongues} or \emph{telepathy} still work, but they cannot cast the spell themselves.
\paragraph*{Casting Spells} They cannot cast spells or concentrate on them.
\paragraph*{Claw Attack} Their unarmed strikes deal 2d4 slashing damage, plus their strength modifier. They count as magical for the purpose of overcoming resistance and immunity to nonmagical attacks and damage. They are proficient with this attack.
\paragraph*{Bite Attack} They can use their action to make a bite attack, dealing 1d8 piercing damage plus their strength modifier. They are proficient with this attack. This attack can also transmit lycanthropy if the target fails a DC 12 Constitution saving throw.
\paragraph*{Feral Surge} The werewolf gains a +2 bonus to attack rolls and damage rolls with their Claw and Bite attacks. This bonus increases to +3 at werewolf level 9 and +4 at level 17.
These bonuses stack with Magic Claw/Bite, Fire Claw/Bite, Ice Claw/Bite, and any other Moon Power that grants a bonus to Claw or Bite attack and damage rolls.
\paragraph*{Strength of the Beast} The werewolf has advantage on Strength checks and Strength saving throws.
\paragraph*{Frenzy} If the werewolf ends their turn in Hybrid Form without having made an attack roll or taken damage since the start of their turn, they must succeed on a Wisdom saving throw (DC 10) or be compelled to shift one step toward Animal Form. On a successful save, the impulse is suppressed until the start of their next turn.

\subsection*{Dire Form}
In this form, the werewolf takes on a look similar to a dire wolf, but larger and more powerful.
The Dire Form is modeled on the dire wolf stat block from D\&D 5e rather than the Hybrid Form: it trades the hybrid's raw combat power for predatory speed, enhanced senses, and pack coordination.
This parallels the original W:tA design, where the Crinos (Hybrid) form was the most combat-effective and the Hispo and Lupus forms prioritized movement, tracking, and wilderness survival.
\paragraph*{Attribute Bonuses} Their Strength increases by 4 from their human form, up to a maximum of 25, and their Dexterity increases by 2, also up to a maximum of 25.
\paragraph*{Damage Resistance} They gain resistance to bludgeoning, piercing, and slashing damage from nonmagical weapons that aren't silvered.
\paragraph*{Vulnerability} Werewolves in all forms have vulnerability to silver and silvered weapons.
\paragraph*{Lack of Speech} They lose the ability to speak, and social checks other than Intimidation are automatically failed.
\paragraph*{Casting Spells} They cannot cast spells or concentrate on them.
\paragraph*{Keen Hearing and Smell} The werewolf has advantage on Wisdom (Perception) checks that rely on hearing and smell.
\paragraph*{Speed} The werewolf's base movement speed increases by 20 feet.
\paragraph*{Bite Attack} They can use their action to make a bite attack, dealing 1d8 piercing damage plus their strength modifier. They are proficient with this attack. This attack can also transmit lycanthropy if the target fails a DC 12 Constitution saving throw. On a hit against a creature, the target must succeed on a DC 13 Strength saving throw or be knocked prone.
\paragraph*{Wilderness Survival} The werewolf has advantage on Wisdom (Survival) checks and on Wisdom (Perception) checks made in natural environments.
\paragraph*{Proficiency Bonus} They gain proficiency with Stealth and Perception skills.

\subsection*{Animal Form}
In this form, the werewolf fully transforms into a wolf.
As with the Dire Form, this form follows the wolf stat block from D\&D 5e: lighter, faster, and built for the hunt rather than direct combat.
\paragraph*{Vulnerability} Werewolves in all forms have vulnerability to silver and silvered weapons.
\paragraph*{Lack of Speech} They lose the ability to speak, and social checks other than Intimidation are automatically failed.
\paragraph*{Casting Spells} They cannot cast spells or concentrate on them.
\paragraph*{Keen Hearing and Smell} The werewolf has advantage on Wisdom (Perception) checks that rely on hearing and smell.
\paragraph*{Speed} The werewolf's base movement speed increases by 10 feet.
\paragraph*{Bite Attack} They can use their action to make a bite attack, dealing 1d4 piercing damage plus their strength modifier. They are proficient with this attack. This attack can also transmit lycanthropy if the target fails a DC 12 Constitution saving throw. On a hit against a creature, the target must succeed on a DC 13 Strength saving throw or be knocked prone.
\paragraph*{Pack Tactics} If they have an ally within 5 feet of their target, they have advantage on attack rolls against that target.
\paragraph*{Wilderness Survival} The werewolf has advantage on Wisdom (Survival) checks and on Wisdom (Perception) checks made in natural environments.

\section*{Blood Inheritance}


When one parent is a hereditary werewolf and the other is an ordinary Medium humanoid, the werewolf parent rolls a d4 at the time of the child's birth.
The result maps directly onto the form spectrum.\footnote{The five forms described above are numbered 1 through 5, from Humanoid to Animal.
Hybrid --- form 3 --- is the middle point, and it is the form a werewolf offspring will naturally inhabit.
This numbering is the key to how hereditary lycanthropy passes from parent to child.}

\begin{DndTable}[header=One Werewolf Parent (roll 1d4)]{XX}
    d4 & Offspring \\
    1  & \textbf{Humanoid.} No lycanthropic traits. The child follows the non-werewolf parent's lineage entirely. \\
    2  & \textbf{Shifter.} Partial inheritance. The child is born with Shifter traits and is part of the bloodline but cannot take the Werewolf Class. \\
    3  & \textbf{Werewolf.} Full hereditary lycanthropy. The child will manifest at the passage into adulthood. \\
    4  & \textbf{Dire Wolf.} Born in and locked to Dire Form. Unable to shift or speak. The tribe raises them as a dire wolf within the pack. \\
\end{DndTable}

When both parents are hereditary werewolves, each rolls a d4 and they add the results.
The sum maps to the same spectrum --- but the minimum result is 2, and any result of 5 or higher produces a wolf pup born wholly in Animal Form.
This means two werewolf parents are \emph{less} likely to produce a werewolf offspring than one werewolf and a human.

\begin{DndTable}[header=Two Werewolf Parents --- roll 2d4 and add]{XX}
    2d4 & Offspring \\
    2   & \textbf{Shifter.} (1 in 16 chance) Partial inheritance. \\
    3   & \textbf{Werewolf.} (2 in 16 chance) Full hereditary lycanthropy. \\
    4   & \textbf{Dire Wolf.} (3 in 16 chance) Born in Dire Form; raised as a dire wolf by the pack. \\
    5–8 & \textbf{Wolf Pup.} (10 in 16 chance) Born in Animal Form. Cannot shift. Raised as an ordinary wolf. \\
\end{DndTable}

A werewolf offspring always inherits the moon of the werewolf parent.
If both parents are werewolves of different moons, the child's moon is determined by whichever of the two moons is fullest at the moment of birth.

A humanoid with contracted lycanthropy counts as an ordinary humanoid for purposes of blood inheritance --- not as a hereditary werewolf.
Only hereditary lycanthropy passes through the bloodline; the contracted curse does not.

\begin{DndSidebar}[float=!b]{The Paradox of Pure Blood}
    The numbers above are not accidental.
    With one werewolf parent, the chance of a werewolf offspring is 1 in 4 (25\%).
    With two werewolf parents, it falls to 2 in 16 (12.5\%).
    Two werewolves together are half as likely to produce a werewolf child as one werewolf and a human.

    This mirrors a theme from \emph{Werewolf: the Apocalypse}: the Garou who breed only among themselves tend to produce damaged or diminished offspring.
    Here the consequence is structural rather than cosmetic --- the bloodline needs humans.
    Werewolves who withdraw entirely from human society will, across generations, produce more wolves than werewolves.
    Their pack thins.
    Their tribe contracts.

    Most tribes therefore discourage werewolf--werewolf pairings not out of sentiment but out of pragmatic necessity.
    The tribe's continuity depends on maintaining bonds with the humanoid communities it is sworn to protect --- and those bonds must be forged in blood as well as custom.

    Dravago and Olarune werewolves are exceptions, though.
    Wolf pups born of Dravago lineage are welcomed rather than mourned.
    The Handling moon's werewolves see no diminishment in a child born wholly wolf --- only a different kind of packmate.
\end{DndSidebar}

\section*{Involuntary Shifting}
Contracted lycanthropes must shift into their Hybrid form during a full moon - only the moon that they are influenced under, not all twelve.
Hereditary lycanthropes do not have to shift - they have control over their shifts.
However, there are certain conditions which can cause them to shift unwillingly:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Taking damage from a silver weapon
    \item Entering the \emph{frightened} condition
    \item \emph{Vicious Mockery} spell
    \item \emph{Tasha's Caustic Brew} spell
    \item Entering a Barbarian rage
    \item DM's discretion: once every full moon
\end{itemize}

If these take place, the lycanthrope must make a Wisdom saving throw.
The DC of the check is 18 - the character level in the werewolf class/2 (rounded down).
On a failed save, they immediately shift into their Hybrid form.
On a successful save, they can choose to resist the shift, and do not need to roll again for 24 hours.
If their moon is full, they have disadvantage on this saving throw.
If their moon is new, (dark moon) they have advantage on this saving throw.
Finally, being under the effect of the spell \emph{Calm Emotions} gives advantage.

A lycanthrope may return from an involuntary shift freely, without a check, using their action on any subsequent turn.
The shift back does not cause exhaustion.

If a lycanthrope is under the effect of the \emph{Moonbeam} spell when involuntary shifting would otherwise trigger, \emph{Moonbeam} takes precedence: the lycanthrope is locked in the form the spell forces and cannot shift until they leave the beam.

\section*{Detecting Werewolves}

\emph{Truesight} reveals werewolves for what they are.
For dramatic effect, consider describing them as superimposed images of the Human, Hybrid and the Animal form.
In addition, the moon that they are associated with is also revealed with \emph{Truesight},
perhaps as an aura, hue, or outright an image of the moon residing in the Ethereal Plane.\par

\begin{DndSidebar}[float=!b]{Detecting Lycanthropes \& 2024 Rules}
    The 2014 rules define \emph{truesight} as letting its bearer ``perceive the original form of a shapechanger or a creature that is transformed by magic,'' which unambiguously reveals a werewolf's true nature.
    The 2024 rules glossary drops the explicit ``shapechanger'' language, granting only the ability to discern the true form of a creature ``that has been transformed by magic.''
    Whether a hereditary werewolf's shift counts as being transformed by magic is left ambiguous even by the 2024 rules' own designers.
    This book rules that it does --- a werewolf's shift is a supernatural gift from Siberys, not a mundane biological trait --- so \emph{Truesight} reveals werewolves under either rule set.
    DMs who prefer a stricter reading of the 2024 wording may rule otherwise.
\end{DndSidebar}

\emph{Zone of Truth} does not reveal a werewolf on its own, but a creature under its effect cannot lie if asked outright whether they are a lycanthrope.
They may still be evasive --- deflecting, refusing to answer, or answering a question that was not quite asked --- but they cannot flatly deny it while affected.
A question like "are you a werewolf" could perhaps be gamed, but a question like "are you or have you ever been a werewolf" or "do you have the ability to change into a werewolf form" is more difficult to evade.\par

\emph{Detect Thoughts} should actually be very revealing:
the spellcaster should realize that there are two ``surface thoughts'' existing side-by-side, one human, one animal.
This makes perfect sense from a dramatic perspective - one of the critical tensions of the werewolves is the dual existence, the internal conflict between human and beast.
It also is a balancing effect on the gameplay.
Eyre werewolves are a notable exception to this rule: see \emph{Eyre, Making}, below.\par

On the magic item and ritual-magic side, this book agrees with the two community-suggested approaches, found on \href{https://rpg.stackexchange.com/questions/182083/is-there-a-spell-or-item-that-can-detect-lycanthropes-while-they-are-in-their-hu}{this discussion on RPG Stack Exchange}.\par

The \emph{Sentinel} minor property (DMG p.143) can be tuned, at the item's creation, to a chosen kind of creature; the item then glows faintly whenever such creatures are within 120 feet of it.
An item tuned to \emph{shapechangers} would reveal a werewolf even in Humanoid Form --- though finding, or commissioning, such an item would likely be a quest in its own right.\par

A \emph{glyph of warding} can be refined to trigger only for a chosen kind of creature, such as shapechangers, and set as a Spell Glyph storing a visible, no-save spell --- \emph{magic missile} is a popular choice --- so that a passing werewolf sets off an unmistakable signal.\par

\begin{DndSidebar}[float=!b]{The Mask Counter-Play}
    \emph{Nystul's magic aura}'s Mask effect changes what a target appears to be to ``spells and magical effects that detect creature type, such as \ldots the trigger of a symbol spell.''
    A \emph{glyph of warding}'s creature-type trigger and a \emph{Sentinel}-tuned item's passive detection both fall squarely into that category, so a werewolf under an active \emph{Nystul's magic aura} slips past both undetected.
    \emph{Truesight} is a different matter: it is not a spell or effect that ``detects creature type'' so much as a direct perception of a creature's true form, so it sees through the Mask regardless.
    This makes \emph{Nystul's magic aura} an appealing tool for an infiltrator werewolf --- reliable against glyphs and wards, but useless against a hunter who has truesight, or who casts \emph{true seeing} on them directly.
    Note that the spell requires a willing target and must be cast up to 24 hours in advance, so it favors premeditated infiltration over a spur-of-the-moment escape.
    Sypheros werewolves eventually gain an innate, always-active version of this Mask effect as a Moon Power (see \emph{Sypheros, Shadow}, below) --- for a hunter relying on wards and tuned items rather than truesight, they are the tribe most likely to walk straight through.
\end{DndSidebar}

\section*{Perspectives}

\emph{Good villains think what they are doing is right. Great villains actually are right.}
-- Michael E. Shea, \href{https://slyflourish.com/lazydm/}{The Lazy Dungeon Master}

From the perspective of most hereditary werewolves, they are guardians of Eberron against the influence of Khyber, with their powers coming from Siberys.
The Inquisition is an organization of radicals and religious zealots.
Yes, it is possible for their powers to be contracted by others, but this is just one of the many influences of Khyber.
This is nowhere near the bloody genocide that the Silver Flame inflicted on the werewolves.\par

From the perspective of the Church of the Silver Flame, a werewolf is a werewolf is a werewolf.
The threat of a werewolf pandemic is ever-present, and this requires vigilance and containment.
Even if the Church were to discover that there are different strains of werewolves, this would not make them any less dangerous to the peoples of Khorvaire.\par

From the perspective of most werewolves, contracted lycanthropy is an abomination, a result of the Khyber influence.\par

Why does all of this matter for your campaign? A good story could involve a recurring Silver Flame villain turning into an ally, perhaps temporarily, at least at first.
Similarly, a PC of the Silver Flame could be presented with this alternative world view from a hereditary werewolf, and can now decide to work with them, or against them.\par

\section*{The Moons}

Moon Powers represent instinctual, supernatural advantages granted by the character's dominant moon.
A character selects one Moon at level 3, as shown in the Werewolf Class table. Additional Moon Powers are gained at levels 6, 10, 14, and 18 --- five features in total over the full class progression.\par

At each of these levels, the player selects one feature from their moon's list.
Features may be taken in any order, subject to their prerequisites: some require a minimum werewolf class level, and some require another feature from the same moon to be taken first.
A character's moon cannot be changed once chosen --- the bond formed at level 3 is permanent, shaped by lineage and the pull of a specific moon on the lycanthrope's blood.\par

Hereditary werewolves understand their moon-bond as part of a broader alignment: they are tied to the moons of Eberron above and instinctively hostile to the corruption of Khyber below.
Aberrations, daelkyr, cults of the Dragon Below, and the influence of Khyber's manifest zones are all viewed with suspicion and often active opposition.
Where the moons cycle and the natural world breathes, Khyber represents the antithesis of what werewolves are called to protect --- and this hostility is felt in the blood, not merely taught.

Each moon is tied to one of Eberron's planes of existence, as shown in the table below.

\begin{DndSidebar}[float=!b]{Werewolves \& Dragonmarked Houses}
    Yes: there is an isomorphism between the moons, the werewolf tribes, and the dragonmarked houses.
    However, the houses have nothing to do with werewolves, and the dragonmarks have nothing to do with the moon features, at least at the surface level.
    Dragonmarked houses working with werewolves does not make sense in the greater Eberron politics, other than small clandestine operations and conspiracies.
    The dragonmarked individuals have different powers than the werewolves.
    Werewolves themselves are not dragonmarked, and have not race- or species- related constraints.
    They are designed to be a distinct add-on on Eberron, not change Eberron's existing structures.\par
    All that said, werewolves having a connection to the Draconic Prophecy may make sense for specific campaigns.
    As usual, the DM knows best.
\end{DndSidebar}

\subsection*{Zarantyr, Storm \& Chaos}

\paragraph*{Perspectives} The balance between chaos and order have to be maintained.
Zarantyr werewolves may identify themselves as the embdoiment of chaos, a counterbalance to Nymm.
Any living thing needs a level of dynamism to survive, and this is what Zarantyr werewolves bring to the table with their powers.

\paragraph*{Common Locations} Zarantyr werewolves are drawn to places where the land itself is unstable or contested --- storm coasts, war-torn borderlands, the wake of natural disasters, or wherever Kythri's chaotic influence bleeds through as sudden squalls and freak weather.
They tend to enjoy being near coasts and mountains, where the winds are strong and storms can be common.
They rarely linger in one place; like the storms they are named for, they arrive without warning, do what needs doing, and move on before the dust settles.
A great option is to have them in Xen'drik, a complex and chaotic land with its colony and the Traveler's Curse.\par

\paragraph*{Origins} Zarantyr werewolves have different tales of their origin. Some believe that they came from Khythri, the churning chaos.
Some tribes claim that their ancestors came from Xen'drik, and have tales of the Shattering.
Others are more philosophical, and they claim a mission to be the bringers of chaos as part of the natural order.\par

\paragraph*{Passive Power \& Traits} Zarantyr werewolves have advantage on all Charisma (Intimidation) checks in all forms.

\paragraph*{Why Does It Matter?} An all-Zarantyr pack suits a campaign that wants its guardians to meet trouble head-on rather than work around it --- front-line brawlers who solve problems with overwhelming force first and ask questions after.
Their volatile, ever-shifting moon gives them a reputation among the other tribes as unpredictable and quick to anger: a useful loose cannon when the party needs a hammer, a liability when the situation calls for restraint.

Consider these NPCs for your game:
\begin{itemize}
    \item A Zarantyr champion who shows up at every local crisis like clockwork, ends it through sheer overwhelming force, and vanishes again before anyone can properly thank them --- treated as a folk legend rather than a person.
    \item A Zarantyr elder fighting against their own tribe's restless nature, trying to hold a normally-itinerant pack in one place to defend a single town that has come to depend on them.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Olarune, Sentinel \& The Twilight Forest}

\paragraph*{Perspectives} Olarune werewolves have stories of themselves as the original werewolves.
They believe that it was first Olarone that gave the gift of Lycanthrophy, and elevated some among the humanoids to be the guardians of Eberron.
They put preserving the original state of Eberron, nature.
They may be concerned about the proliferation of human and humanoid cities.
Some among them may not differentiate much between populous human settlements and the Demonoid Wastes.
Some others may consider themselves to be a counter-balance to the Eyre werewolves.
And some others may just want to protect a ``territory'' from any unnatural influence, whether that be mining, farming, or corruption from Khyber.\par

\paragraph*{Common Locations} Olarune werewolves are drawn to the oldest, most untouched wilderness --- ancient forests, sacred groves, and the borders where civilization presses up against land that has never been cleared --- with packs most often found near manifest zones touching Lamannia, the Twilight Forest, where the wild feels most alert to the presence of trespassers.
They mark and hold territory the way other guardians hold a post: not to claim it, but to keep it whole.
They are likely to be encountered in and around The Towering Wood, from its edges to its deepest wilderness.
Or any other wood, ranging from the Eldritch Grove to the King's Forest.\par

\paragraph*{Origins} Olarune werewolves believe themselves to be the original werewolves.
Their stories speak of Olarune gifting them lycanthropy as a weapon against Khyber, to preserve balance on Eberron, and they see themselves as the truest protectors of nature and the wild among the twelve tribes.

\paragraph*{Passive Power \& Traits} Olarune werewolves cannot be surprised while in Hybrid, Dire, or Animal form, and have advantage on Wisdom (Perception) checks to detect hidden creatures in all forms.

\paragraph*{Why Does It Matter?} An all-Olarune pack suits a campaign about defending a specific, sacred place --- an ancient grove, a Lamannia manifest zone, or the last untouched stretch of wilderness before a city's expansion reaches it.
Their command of plant magic and their claim to being the first werewolves make them natural anchors for a campaign about territory, trespass, and what it costs to hold a line that keeps eroding.

Consider these NPCs for your game:
\begin{itemize}
    \item An Olarune sentinel who has warded a sacred grove with generations of \emph{Entangle} and \emph{Wall of Thorns}, now forced to decide whether to tear down her own wards when the only way through is for an ally who genuinely needs to reach what lies on the other side.
    \item A young Olarune werewolf whose ancestral territory has been swallowed by a growing city, left guarding a single overgrown lot as though it were still an ancient forest.
    \item A pack of werewolves who fought as guerillas against Aundair for the secession of the Eldeen Reaches.
    \item Some Brelish miners in are digging a little bit too deep in Graywall Mountains. A pack of Olarune werewolves want to protect the land from the horror that the miner awoke. Silver Flame inquisitors want to protect the villagers from both the Khyber influence and the werewolves. Who does the party side with, or how do they handle the situation?
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Eyre: Making, Humanoid Coexitence \& Fire}

\paragraph*{Common Locations} Werewolves under the auspices of the moon Eyre are drawn to human and humanoid settlements and cities rather than wilderness --- forge-quarters, foundries, and crowded lower wards, with likely packs beneath Sharn itself, close to the manifest zone touching Fernia, the Sea of Fire.
Their survival strategy sets them apart from every other tribe: not isolation, but proximity --- staying close enough to the people they guard that they can act the moment they are needed.

\paragraph*{Origins} Eyre werewolves do not have a separate origin story.
They tend to think of themselves as humans or humanoids, not a distinct group.
They just have a different\ldots trait then their neighbours, a certain ability that may be misunderstood by the society at large.
So, a Brelish Eyre werewolf is likely to consider themselves as a Brelish individual.

\paragraph*{Passive Power \& Traits} All werewolves of Eyre can use \emph{Control Flames} and \emph{Create Bonfire} at will, in any form, requiring no components.
These function as the cantrip versions of the spells.

Eyre werewolves also have advantage on saving throws against \emph{detect thoughts}, and any surface thoughts read from them come through as those of an ordinary human or humanoid, not the doubled man-and-wolf presence typical of other tribes (see \emph{Detecting Werewolves}, above).
This follows naturally from a tribe that does not experience itself as divided in the first place: an Eyre werewolf who thinks of themselves as simply Brelish, or simply Sharn-born, has nothing doubled for a prying mind to find.

Finally, Eyre werewolves, in the Shifter Form, cannot be found out as werewolves, by any checks (see \nameref{sec:shifter-form}, above).
Unless seen \emph{while shifting (i.e. transforming)}, they are completely unnoticeable as anything other than just Longtooth Shifter characters.
There are no tell-tale signs that something is amiss, even to the trained eye, even to actual Shifters.\par

\paragraph*{Why Does It Matter?} An all-Eyre pack suits a campaign rooted in a city rather than a wilderness --- guardians who watch over forges, foundries, and crowded streets instead of forests, and whose enemies are as likely to be a runaway magical device or a fire-setting cult as a wild beast.
Their comfort with flame and with crowds makes them a natural fit for adventures set in Sharn or any other industrial city, where the danger is built rather than born.

Consider these NPCs for your game:
\begin{itemize}
    \item A foundry overseer who quietly patrols Sharn's factory district in Hybrid Form after hours, making sure the fires that power the city stay controlled rather than become a danger to it --- an Eyre werewolf who treats flame as infrastructure to protect, not a weapon to wield.
    \item A young Eyre werewolf, born and raised entirely within a city, who has never set foot in true wilderness and finds the countryside as alien and unsettling as most other tribes find the streets of Sharn.
    \item A pack of fully-armoued werewolves spewing fire from their mouths\ldots You didin't know that that is what your campaign needed, did you?
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Dravago: Handling, Animal Coexistence \& Cold}

\paragraph*{Origins} Dravago werewolves do not think of themselves as having an origin story. Rather, they have a tendency to think of themselves as animals who can fake being a human or a humanoid.

Consider these NPCs for your game:

\subsection*{Rhaan: Thelanis \& The Fae}

\paragraph*{Common Locations} Rhaan werewolves are most commonly found near Thelanian manifest zones, where the boundary between Eberron and Thelanis, the Faerie Court, grows thin --- places where moonlight has a quality of its own and the veil between story and flesh is at its most permeable.
In his \href{https://keith-baker.com/2025-lycanthropes/}{2025 article on lycanthropes}, Keith Baker identifies a category he calls \textbf{The Changing Folk}: fey beings who shift freely between humanoid and bestial form through a fey inheritance, and who prefer to remain in isolation within Thelanian manifest zones rather than engage with the outer world.
This book draws on that vision for the Rhaan tribe. Their moon-bond has a distinctly fey character, their abilities touch the world of nature, story, and spirit, and their packs are as likely to be found beside a moonlit manifest zone as in a conventional wilderness.

\paragraph*{Origins} Rhaan werewolves believe themselves to have originated from Thelanis.

\paragraph*{Passive Power \& Traits} Rhaan werewolves have \emph{Fey Ancestry}, however, this comes up only in Hybrid and Dire forms.
In other words, any spells or powers that would reveal \emph{Fey Ancestry} would do so only in these two forms.
\emph{Fey Ancestry} is different between 2014 and 2024 rules, use whichever one you use for your campaign.

\paragraph*{Why Does It Matter?} An all-Rhaan pack works well as the backbone of a campaign built around a Thelanian manifest zone under strain --- an archfey's bargain going sour, a seasonal rite that must be completed exactly right, or a mortal settlement that has expanded too close to a fey crossing without knowing what it borders.
Their story-and-nature magic (\emph{Speak with Animals}, \emph{Summon Fey}, \emph{Conjure Woodland Beings}) rewards campaigns built on bargains, omens, and the cost of a broken promise, rather than straightforward dungeon-crawling.

Consider these NPCs for your game:
\begin{itemize}
    \item A trusted courtier, guide, or hedge witch who has walked alongside the party through an otherwise purely fey-flavored adventure --- seemingly just another native of Thelanis --- until the moment they need to fight for the party, when they shift to Hybrid or Dire Form and reveal themselves as a Rhaan werewolf who has been quietly testing, or guarding, the party the whole time. A ready-made twist for slotting a hereditary werewolf into a campaign that never mentioned lycanthropy.
    \item A Rhaan elder whose Humanoid Form is a respected go-between at an archfey's court, but who sheds that face entirely once back among their own pack, and finds the two halves of that life harder to reconcile with each passing year.
    \item A Rhaan pack matriarch, most often encountered already in Hybrid Form, who judges outsiders by how they react to seeing a wolf-shaped person mid-conversation rather than by anything they actually say.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Sypheros: Shadow \& The Undeath}
% Mabar, the Endless Night - New phase (not full) when Mabar is close. Most intense during Long Shadows when The Shadow reigns.
% WRITING PROMPT: Bone Shadows - Occultists and shamans, spirit politics, metaphysics, shadowed truths; Shadow Lords - Political manipulators, power, control

\paragraph*{Common Locations} Sypheros werewolves are most often found near manifest zones touched by Mabar, the Endless Night --- crypts and barrow-fields, the long dusk of forgotten crossroads, and places like Malleon's Gate in Sharn, where the boundary to the Endless Night runs thin.
They do not linger there out of kinship with what stirs in the dark, but to stand watch over it --- the same way a Rhaan pack might keep a moonlit clearing: not a shrine, but a post.

\paragraph*{Origins} Sypheros werewolves do not have a common origin story. Different clans and packs may hold different beliefs. Some may believe themselves to be tasked with guarding life on Eberron from the undead. Others may believe themselves to be balancing influence between Khyber and Siberys, and life and undeath. Others may be associated with some mystery cults. This is a tribe of \emph{shadows}, and the lack of clarity in their origin story reflects that.
Most hereditary werewolves distrust Mabar and are deeply wary of necromancy and the powers of undeath --- forces that corrupt and subvert the natural cycle rather than serve it --- and Sypheros werewolves occupy an uncomfortable position among the twelve tribes as a result.
Their moon is aligned with Mabar's influence; they walk in shadow and darkness as naturally as other werewolves walk in moonlit clearings.
Other tribes are often suspicious of them because of this association, and a Sypheros pack may need to work harder than most to earn trust within the broader community of hereditary werewolves.
Their affinity for shadow is a tool in service of Eberron's guardianship --- not a submission to the dark --- but that distinction is not always easy to demonstrate, and not all tribes are willing to accept it.

\paragraph*{Passive Power \& Traits} When a Sypheros werewolf shifts into Hybrid Form, each creature of their choice within 30 feet that can see them must succeed on a Wisdom saving throw (DC 8 + proficiency bonus + Charisma modifier) or become \emph{frightened} of the werewolf until the end of their next turn.
A creature that succeeds on this saving throw is immune to this effect from that werewolf for 24 hours.

\paragraph*{Why Does It Matter?} An all-Sypheros pack suits a campaign about mistrust and the work of proving yourself --- guardians whose own community treats them as suspect by default, who must work twice as hard to be believed, and who sometimes wonder if the suspicion is entirely wrong.
Their shadow magic and necrotic resistance fit naturally into intrigue set around Karrnath's crypts, a Blood of Vol congregation, an Emerald Claw plot, or a Mabar manifest zone where something restless has started to stir.
Create a pack of werewolves fighting in Karrnath against the Blood of Vol, perhaps working with the Royal Eyes of Aundair?

Consider these NPCs for your game:
\begin{itemize}
    \item A Sypheros werewolf working undercover inside a Blood of Vol congregation to gather intelligence for their pack, whose cover is wearing dangerously thin.
    \item A Sypheros werewolf who, during the long nights locals call \emph{Long Shadows}, is approached by a cult of the Shadow, one of the Dark Six, who mistake the werewolf's Mabar-aligned power for kinship and try to recruit them. The werewolf must publicly --- and sometimes violently --- prove that an affinity for shadow serves Eberron's guardianship, not the Dark Six.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Planned Expansions}

The twelve tribes detailed in this book are werewolves, bound to Eberron's moons and Siberys' guardianship --- but Keith Baker's lycanthropes are wider than wolves alone, and this book intends to grow to meet them.

\textbf{Weretigers} are planned next, prowling the deep jungles of Xen'drik, and the untamed jungle reaches of Darguun, Valenar, and the Talenta Plains --- solitary guardians suited to country too wild and too dangerous for a pack to hold.

\textbf{Werebears} are also planned, holding the high peaks of the Mror Holds and the deep old-growth forests of the Eldeen Reaches --- solitary protectors in the same vein as weretigers, but suited to cold heights and ancient woodland rather than jungle.

\textbf{Wererats} are planned as well, at home in the crowded undercities of Stormreach and Sharn --- guardians whose territory is not open wilderness but the tunnels, sewers, and forgotten spaces beneath civilization's greatest cities.

None of these strains are bound to the twelve moons the way werewolves are, and each will need its own guardian tradition rather than simply borrowing this book's lunar framework.
Expect them in a future edition of this book.
